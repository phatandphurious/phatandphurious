Live Report · 1 & 2 August 2026 · Parc des Expositions, Pérols · 7 min read

Family Piknik 2026,
fifteen years at home.

For once, no train and no plane. The festival we were covering sits twenty minutes from our door, and it was turning fifteen. Two days at the Parc des Expositions with Armand Van Helden rolling out the classics, Hysta bringing the Hard Stage to its knees, Étienne de Crécy and his legendary denim shirt, Blond:ish as the revelation of Sunday and Rebekah to finish deep in the red.

By Phat and Lolita · Photos: Phat & Phurious FM

A festival we have watched grow for fifteen years

There is something particular about covering a festival that happens on your doorstep. No travel logistics, no jet lag, just the L3 tram out to Pérols and the strange feeling of watching artists you have followed for years turn up in your own back garden. Family Piknik turned fifteen on 1 and 2 August, and for the first time we were there on a press pass.

The format has changed scale but kept its backbone: four stages, more than sixty artists, from midday to two in the morning. The Elrow Stage for the party House, the Hard Stage set up indoors in one of the park halls, the Nomad Stage under its wooden frame for the sharper selections, and the Scène Tremplin handing slots to talent from the region. Each one with its own crowd, its own tempo, its own temperature.

Saturday: Armand Van Helden, guest star of the Elrow Stage

We open at the Hard Stage with 25EMEHEURE at 6pm. The Parisian, a member of the Organik collective, delivers frontal, percussive hard techno, with that taste for vocals that sets her apart from most of what gets produced right now. The hall is already well filled at that hour, which says a lot about the crowd's appetite for harder music.

25EMEHEURE @ Hard Stage and the Elrow performers - Family Piknik 2026 · Photos: Phat & Phurious FM

Over to the Elrow Stage for 6.30pm. A pure product of the New York House scene, Armand Van Helden comes to this part of France so rarely! The anticipation was enormous, and you could feel it in the density of the crowd well before his slot. For close to an hour and a half he rolls through Funk and Disco leaning House classics, without ever reaching for a cheap trick. Nothing but hits, played masterfully.

The flip side is that a set built entirely on classics takes no risks. We knew what we were getting, and it was exactly what we came for. Anyone hoping to hear him dig deeper, or pull two or three rarities from his own catalogue, went home wanting.

One detail we could hardly miss, given he had his back to us for a good part of the set: he was wearing a Björk t-shirt, her portrait on the front and the chorus of Big Time Sensuality printed across the back. “I don't know my future after this weekend, and I don't want to.” We are still looking for a better programme for a Saturday night at a festival!

His crowd-pleasing tracklist combined with the carnival atmosphere Elrow brings with it was pure madness. For anyone who has never experienced an Elrow party: expect spectacular staging, done properly. Dozens of unhinged performers (actors, acrobats, costumed characters) take over the stage, wander through the site and interact with the crowd. Add the giant mascots and the tons of confetti. The party is immersive, intensely visual, and the atmosphere is unlike anything else.

Except the crowd held its own. A friend of ours was walking around with an inflatable paddling pool for trousers, a yellow rubber duck sitting in it and a bubble gun in each hand. Elsewhere on site, an entire group in nothing but swim briefs, and a man in a feathered headdress crossing the crowd without anyone batting an eye. Elrow sets the bar high on costumes, and we have seen crowds put in a lot less effort to keep up.

Armand Van Helden @ Elrow Stage - Family Piknik 2026 · Photo: Phat & Phurious FM

Saturday night, running between stages

Back to the Hard Stage for Todiefor at 7pm, then Creeds at 8pm. In between, a detour to the Nomad Stage for another pillar of the French electronic scene: Kiko b2b Lucye! A Family Piknik regular, Kiko delivers with Lucye one of the most solid sets of Saturday. Intense and perfectly controlled. A format that plays to how well they complement each other rather than having them talk over one another.

At 8.45pm the Scène Tremplin hosted IRINA, playing hypnotic groove techno. The room is tiny and it was well filled, which is no small thing at that hour with three other stages running at full tilt outside. This is exactly the kind of slot a live report should never skip: these stages are where local careers get built. And when it is your own region, you watch them differently. In two years those names will be on the posters we walk past in town.

Todiefor, Creeds and Kiko b2b Lucye - Family Piknik 2026 · Photos: Phat & Phurious FM

The Nomad Stage, a refuge for the curious

On the Nomad Stage by Afterseason, Family Piknik went for a more intimate, curious programme aimed at electronic music heads. A resolutely underground approach, under a wooden frame cut by a red neon. The contrast with the excess of Elrow is striking, and that is exactly what makes the site worth walking around.

Étienne de Crécy played there at 9pm, denim shirt on his back like a signature. A set that is always elegant and unifying, with no spectacular effects. An artist with a strong musical identity, he reminded everyone why the French Touch pioneer remains an essential reference. And he did not stop at the scheduled time: he kept the set going while Brina Knauss settled in comfortably, joined on the decks by Erol Alkan, who came up to lend a hand. That unplanned b2b delighted more than a few!

Brina Knauss also left her mark on the Nomad Stage, with a set that fitted the melodic, immersive identity of the place perfectly. Blending Melodic House and atmospheric techno, she offered a selection built entirely on finesse, carried by gradual builds and carefully worked textures.

Étienne de Crécy and Brina Knauss @ Nomad Stage - Family Piknik 2026 · Photos: Phat & Phurious FM

At midnight, Erol Alkan comes back to the same stage, this time with Roman Flügel, for what will probably stand as one of the sharpest sets of the whole programme. A demanding selection and a rare sense of musical storytelling. Their musical closeness goes back a long way, to around the 2010s, and Roman Flügel has released a few EPs on Phantasy, Erol Alkan's label, where he is one of the key artists. You can hear it.

On one side, Roman Flügel, known for his eclecticism. He moves with great fluidity from minimal House to hypnotic techno, often folding in IDM, acid or more experimental textures. On the other, Erol Alkan, who likes to mix techno, electro, cosmic disco, krautrock, breaks and psychedelic cuts. The two share several affinities: long transitions, an organic progression through the set rather than drops and abrupt breaks, a taste for rare records, exclusive edits and revisited classics. That is precisely why their b2b works so well!

A set praised for its eclecticism and its depth, running against the more conventional formats of the big festivals.

Roman Flügel b2b Erol Alkan @ Nomad Stage - Family Piknik 2026 · Photo: Phat & Phurious FM

Hysta, or the art of fooling everyone

Midnight too at the Hard Stage, and this is our slap of Saturday. It is also the one flaw we will hold against this programme: Hysta and the Roman Flügel / Erol Alkan b2b started on the same minute, on opposite stages. Two of the finest moments of the weekend, and no festival-goer can be in both places. On a bill this dense, that kind of trade-off stings.

Hysta arrives all smiles, looking perfectly relaxed, and for an hour she drops atomic strikes. The gap between that easy demeanour and the violence of the sound is total, the crowd is not fooled for a second, and the main room is packed to the walls.

Space 92 closes a Saturday that never comes down

Saturday closes at 12.30am on the Elrow Stage with Space 92, hockey jersey on his back. At that hour you expect the site to empty out slowly and the last stragglers to hang on out of principle. Exactly the opposite happened: the crowd was in a frenzy, the energy went up a notch instead of dropping, and the Elrow staging fired off its last rounds of confetti as if it were a quarter to midnight. The Frenchman held his crowd to the very end before sending everybody to bed in disarray!

Hysta @ Hard Stage and Space 92 @ Elrow Stage - Family Piknik 2026 · Photos: Phat & Phurious FM

Sunday: family day

Sunday changes colour, at least on part of the site. Fewer kicks, more groove, a crowd in the mood to dance. The Hard Stage, on the other hand, was hitting just as hard as the night before: the day was not softer, it was more contrasted. Chelina Manuhutu opens our day at 6pm on the Elrow Stage. Born in Amsterdam to an Indonesian father and a Dutch mother, now based in Ibiza, she has built her name on a groovy tech house shot through with Afro and Latin percussion. On paper, an early evening slot. In practice, everyone was already moving within ten minutes. The kind of set that sets the tone for a day.

Meanwhile, Xandy was holding the Scène Tremplin. At the Hard Stage, now curated by Procrastination, Blakeys takes over at 7pm. The Frenchman is a regular with the collective, touring with them often, and he delivers a raw, galloping acid rave, saturated to the core. At 7pm on a Sunday the hall could have filled up quietly. It was already full.

At 8pm, CEBB takes the Nomad Stage, flying that day under the United x Paillote Paradise banner, as good as playing at home. CEBB has been a safe bet since the 2000s, with a musical signature you recognise in his sets straight away. His house selection, minimal and melodic, fitted the hour, the place and the light perfectly, just before sunset, right in the golden hour!

Chelina Manuhutu @ Elrow Stage and CEBB @ Nomad Stage - Family Piknik 2026 · Photos: Phat & Phurious FM

Blond:ish, the revelation of Sunday

Blond:ish at 9pm, someone we only knew by name. An hour later we were sold, and we were not the only ones: she won over the whole Elrow Stage. A well-built set moving between melodic House, a touch of Pop and Afro rhythms, recalling the Defected aesthetic. She kept the momentum going throughout, and on every build the oversized Elrow staging picked up the baton. A selection at once sharp, accessible and resolutely feel good. Blond:ish, a safe bet.

Blond:ish @ Elrow Stage - Family Piknik 2026 · Photo: Phat & Phurious FM

Rebekah, a pioneer of uncompromising industrial techno

Late in the evening at the Hard Stage. William Luck warms the room at 10pm, and that is putting it mildly. The Parisian has made his name on a brutal, visceral hard techno fed by underground rave culture and early 2000s schranz. He laid the ground without ever easing off.

Then Rebekah arrives at 11pm and completely changes the nature of the place. Behind a classy stage presence hides a genuine striking force, sitting between industrial techno and hard dance.

Her uncompromising set makes its mark through sheer power and precision, turning the stage and the dancefloor into a field of expression and detonation. Because yes, musical intensity is not measured in BPM alone, but also in the personality and the vision of the artist carrying it. A performance that commands respect. She clearly brought another dimension to the Hard Stage, where most of the day's sets were chasing immediate impact. A signature recognisable from the opening bars, confirming why Rebekah is an iconic figure of the genre.

William Luck and Rebekah @ Hard Stage - Family Piknik 2026 · Photos: Phat & Phurious FM

The Hard Stage, the real surprise of this edition

If there is one trend to take away from this weekend, it is this one. A programme of devastating kicks, and a main room packed across both days of the festival, from start to finish, with a crowd that knew the tracks and had come specifically for them. The huge appetite for harder music is no longer a niche, it is a groundswell, and Family Piknik read it right by handing the programming to Organik on Saturday and Procrastination on Sunday.

Hard Stage - Family Piknik 2026 · Photo: Phat & Phurious FM

Our verdict on Family Piknik 2026

Let us talk about what makes or breaks a festival in the Hérault in August: the heat. It was extreme on both days. The organisers had installed misting fans all over the site and that changes everything, we kept coming back to them between sets.

The weak point of the site is the Hard Stage hall. No air conditioning, a main room full two days running, and a heat that became genuinely hard to bear late in the evening. We are willing to believe aircon would not have been enough over such a surface, but there is a real issue here for future editions, all the more so as the festival is betting more and more on this stage.

Credit where it is due for the self-service beer dispensers scattered around the site: the wait was reasonable everywhere, and the price gentler than at the bar, 8 euros for 40cl against 10 euros at the counter. Still expensive, but in line with what festivals charge these days.

On the food truck side, wide choice and real quality. We tasted a fair bit of it, with no shame whatsoever: Belgian food, smoked barbecue, Italian ice cream, churros for dessert. Count between 5 and 15 euros a dish depending on what you go for. This is a point where plenty of festivals settle for the bare minimum. Clearly not here.

Fifteen years in, the festival feels like it has found its balance: big enough to bring in Armand Van Helden and Spanish collective Elrow; sharp enough to offer a demanding Nomad Stage with sets to match; bold enough to hand the hard style programming to the leading French collectives of the genre; and a newcomer stage to put the rookies in the spotlight. One thing is certain on home turf, we will be there next year!
